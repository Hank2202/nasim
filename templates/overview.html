The Neutral Spire & The Great Convergence

Three centuries after the cataclysmic tectonic upheaval known as the Great Convergence, continental plates fused inward, elevating the obsidian cliffs of the Neutral Spire. Around this central sanctum lie six distinct sovereign nations with fundamentally asymmetrical endowments—ranging from fertile agrarian lowlands and volcanic smelters to maritime trade straits and abyssal mining chasms.

With harvest shortfalls looming, the six sovereign delegations have convened at the Spire of Accords for a decisive three-turn diplomatic summit. Every nation must navigate a demanding Triple Mandate:

I. Domestic Survival

Prevent famine at all costs. Populations consume {{ grain }} Alchemical Grain every turn ({{ grain_efficient }} if Technological Efficiency is unlocked). Silos reaching 0 trigger severe civil starvation riots ({{ starving_penalty }}).

II. Continental Governance

Use your Assembly votes to pass resolutions, such as shared grain, sanctions on the richest and border guards, to shape continental order.

III. Classified Directives

{% if rules.one_secret_mission %}Each nation has one secret mission{% else %}Each delegate brings a secret mission{% endif %}, visible only on its delegation's phones. Every mission, public or secret, counts the same when the summit ends.

Simulation Chronology (3 Turns)

The simulation runs through 3 operational turns. Each turn combines freeform bilateral negotiations and formal parliamentary plenary sessions:

Phase 1: Working & Bilateral Trade Session ({{ working_minutes }} Minutes) ACTIVE ROOM FLOOR

Delegates move freely through the room conducting face-to-face negotiations. On their phones, delegations trade, enact decrees{% if rules.quiz %}, read the Spireopedia and answer its quiz for resources{% endif %}, and table motions for the Assembly.

Phase 2: The Assembly Votes PARLIAMENTARY ROLL-CALL

Motions with a co-sponsor go to the vote. Nations cast weighted votes (1, 2 or 3, and 1 more for the richest). If the votes for outnumber the votes against, the motion takes effect at once.{% if rules.grand_plenary %} In Turn 3 the Grand Plenary debates and votes on one motion at a time.{% else %} Every turn ends with the same single vote.{% endif %}

Phase 3: Turn Processing & Socioeconomic Settlement ENGINE EXECUTION

The game deducts grain consumption ({{ grain }} per turn in this summit, a quarter less with Technological Efficiency), applies starvation penalties if silos empty, gives the bonus vote to the wealthiest nation, and publishes the news.

Turn 3 Finale: Summit Concluded & Debriefing STRATEGIC REVELATION

After the last vote of Turn 3 nations eat one last time and the summit ends. Secret missions are revealed, the continent's verdict and the honours are announced, and the rankings go up on the projector.

{% if rules.verdict_rules %}
  • 🟢 Stable continent: at the end no nation is out of Grain, every nation has at least 50 Stability and the average Standing of all nations is at least 50. No nation's Stability may ever have fallen to 0.
  • 🟡 Fragile peace: at the end at most one nation is out of Grain, and no nation's Stability has ever fallen to 0.
  • 🔴 Continental crisis: two or more nations are out of Grain at the end, or a nation's Stability fell to 0.
{% endif %}

Socioeconomic Algorithms & Formulas

What the game calculates at each turn settlement:

Sovereign Assembly & Dynamic Voting Weights
Solaris: 3 Votes (Superpower)
Sylvan, Val-Khor, Aquila, Aethelgard: 2 Votes
Umbra: 1 Vote (Isolated Enclave)
+1 Bonus Vote for Continental Plutocrat

Base parliamentary votes reflect each nation's constitutional standing, with an extra vote awarded each turn to the continent's wealthiest nation.

Baseline Consumption & Famine
Turn Consumption = -{{ grain }} Grain
With Tech Efficiency = -{{ grain_efficient }} Grain (-25% cut)
If Grain ≤ 0: {{ starving_penalty }}

Civil populations consume grain every turn. Running out of grain triggers immediate domestic starvation riots.

Technological Efficiency
Threshold: Aether Cores ≥ 75
Effect: Permanent -25% Grain Consumption

Industrializing with 75+ active aether matrices unlocks automation that permanently cuts food consumption from {{ grain }} to {{ grain_efficient }}.

Plutocratic Drift
Condition: Highest Tangible Wealth (Grain+Cores+Silk)
Reward: +1 Assembly Vote (only while it is the single wealthiest)

The single wealthiest nation gains an extra vote from sheer financial clout.

Qualitative Fog of War Tiers
≥ 60: HIGH (Abundant / Formidable)
30–59: MEDIUM (Operational / Balanced)
< 30: LOW (Scarce / Vulnerable)

Projector and foreign registries display clean qualitative tiers (LOW, MEDIUM, HIGH) rather than exact values to preserve negotiation tension and bluffing.

Spireopedia & the Lore Quiz

The Spireopedia is the game's built-in, Wikipedia-style encyclopaedia of the Continent: the history of all six nations, its wars, treaties, geography and resources. Delegates read it in the Wiki tab of their phone console and answer questions in the separate Quiz tab.

  • The quiz is a timed run, one question at a time. A nation sees the stake and the topic, presses Start when it is ready, and then has {{ settings.quiz_seconds }} seconds to answer. There is no pause, no skipping once it has started, and no pointer to the article.
  • Every nation meets the same {{ quiz_count }} questions in its own random order; about a third unlock each turn. The stake of each question is random, from 1 to 9 (5 on average).
  • Correct answer: the stake in Grain, Cores or Silk.
  • Wrong answer or no answer in time: loses the same resource and amount, and the question is locked for that nation.
  • A nation's delegates share one open question. The news only carries a summary of the quiz every five minutes, never who answered what.

War

From the turn the facilitator allows (Turn 2 by default) a nation can attack another nation from the War room on its phone, once per turn. The army decides most of the fight, but not all of it:

  • Strength = Military × readiness × (1 + ground and weather + luck).
  • Readiness (about 0.35 to 1.9) comes from Stability, Grain, Cores, Vitality, Standing and Silk. The further a value is from 50 the harder it pulls: a starving, unstable nation fights far worse, a fed and calm one far better.
  • Ground and weather: the defender's home ground (mountains and islands help it, open fields hurt it) and the weather of the turn, shown to everyone, add or take a few percent. Luck is a little more. All of this is smaller than the army and the readiness.
  • The scouts give the attacker a blurred estimate of the odds; other nations' exact numbers stay secret.
  • Result: the loser loses soldiers, Stability and Vitality and a part of its Grain, Cores and Silk goes to the winner (some is lost in the fighting). The bigger the win, the more is taken. Attacking always costs supplies (10 Grain) and Standing, and a lost attack costs more.
  • A battle is announced on the news and pops up on the projector with an animation.

Hardware Deployment & Workshop Roles

The simulation runs across three kinds of screen, either on a local Wi-Fi network or a hosted server:

Participant Screen

Mobile Cockpit (/realm/<id>)

Students scan the QR code and log in with their delegation passcode. The tabs:

Realm: Resources, metrics, domestic decrees, secret directives.
World: Live news wire and the continental registry.
Trade: Bilateral resource offers.
Motions: {% if rules.free_motions %}Draft{% else %}Table{% endif %} and co-sponsor resolutions.
Wiki: The Spireopedia (reading only).{% if rules.quiz %}
Quiz: Timed lore questions, one at a time.{% endif %}
Auditorium Display

Projector Screen (/projector)

Displayed on room projector. High-visibility zero-scroll design:

• Giant turn clock and active session indicator.
• Continental Diplomatic Standing board with Fog of War tiers.
• Live streaming news ticker with synthesized audio cues.
• QR code with the joining address for fast phone onboarding.
• Full-screen parliamentary roll-call visualizer during votes.
Facilitator Laptop

Command Deck (/admin)

For the person running the game:

• What is going on, what comes next, and the button for it.
• The clock: start, pause, adjust.
• News, random events and crises by hand.
• Every nation's numbers, with undo.
• Passcodes, seats and passcode cards to print.
• Saves, and the way back after a server restart.
